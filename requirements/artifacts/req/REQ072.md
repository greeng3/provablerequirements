Whether a claim can be lowered is not a property of its wording; it depends on what there is
to lower to — the observables the subject declares — and on what each category's engine can
express. A classifier asked that question while shown only prose is guessing at both halves,
and the measured failure mode is exactly the guess going wrong: a lowerable claim read as
observation-only, refuted by the subject's own declarations.

Where the tool delegates triage to a language model, the prompt carries what the question
needs. It names the subject's declared observables — the predicate-shaped and sort-shaped
names an adapter could bind — and it states the category boundaries produced from the same
encoding the gate enforces, not restated in parallel prose that can drift from it. What does
not fit is truncated openly: a list that was cut says so and says how much was omitted.

The classification stays advisory and the failure rules for unusable replies are unchanged;
this requirement governs what the question contains, not what the answer is allowed to claim.
