A checker can be unable to run for a reason that lies in the checker rather than in the subject or
its formalization: what the checker is welded to may be older than what the subject's own
dependencies require. The tool must report that as the thing it is.

Reported as the raw failure of the underlying build, it invites the operator to act — to change the
subject, to add an annotation, to adjust a dependency — when no action of theirs can succeed.
Separating "this checker cannot be applied here" from "the claim could not be established here" is
the same honesty the verdict vocabulary exists for, applied to the reason rather than to the answer.

Such a checker is not unwired. An inconclusive answer from a wired checker costs nothing and stays
truthful. What must stop is counting it as coverage: a checker that cannot run against a subject
contributes no evidence about it, and any tally that still treats it as an available source of
evidence overstates what has been checked.

Recognition is grounded in failures actually observed rather than in what such a failure is presumed
to look like, and each recognized signature is placed where it can neither be pre-empted by a
coarser reading of the same output nor mask a run that succeeded.
