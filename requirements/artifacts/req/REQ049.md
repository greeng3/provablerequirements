A verdict is only about the environment that produced it. Two verdicts produced under different
verification toolchains are different claims, so a stored verdict records the environment it was
proved in, and a verdict whose recorded environment no longer matches the current one is reported
as drifted, in the same terms as every other way a verdict goes stale.

The recorded environment is the one the tool actually ran in, never the one the subject declares
it offers. Those two can differ, and treating a declared environment as the producing one would
attribute a verdict to a place it was never established.

Because the environment cannot be established fully or reliably by inspection, the record is
assembled from parts of differing strength and is explicit about which is which. An operator may
declare a stable name for their environment; the tool detects what it genuinely can, such as the
versions the verification engines report; and it may note coarse context that identifies nothing
on its own. Only the parts that are both stable and meaningful participate in deciding drift.

A component whose identity cannot be established is recorded as unestablished and excluded from
the comparison, never folded in as though its identity were known. This is required in both
directions: an unestablished identity must not silently manufacture drift, and it must not be
presented as though the environment were fully captured. Where the record cannot see a change,
it says so.

A verdict stored before the tool recorded environments carries none, and is left alone rather
than reported as drifted on a basis that cannot be established. No claim about the environment is
recorded that the tool cannot substantiate from where it runs.
