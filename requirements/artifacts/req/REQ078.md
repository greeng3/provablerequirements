When a requirement's verdict is first reached or changes, provreq records a durable, human-visible breadcrumb on the requirement itself, alongside the formalization admission, so a reader of the requirement sees not only that it was formalized but whether the formalization holds. The breadcrumb names the verdict's status, its basis, and whether the correspondence is mechanical or asserted, with the time it was recorded. It is appended only on the first verdict and when the verdict changes — in its status, its basis, or its correspondence: a re-verify that reaches the identical verdict adds nothing, because the record is an event stream, not a per-run trace. A change in basis or correspondence counts even when the status holds steady, so an upgrade a reader cares about — a bounded `holds` becoming a proven one, or a mechanical correspondence becoming asserted — leaves its own breadcrumb rather than being swallowed because the status did not move. The breadcrumb is written only where the requirement source keeps a review log; a source without one is unaffected, and the verdict stays recorded in provreq's own store regardless.
