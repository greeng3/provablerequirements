`verify` records a verdict (REQ039) and moves on; nothing blocks a change that regresses a previously-proven requirement to unknown or refuted, so such a regression lands silently. The gate is the enforcement boundary that closes that gap: an audit tool becomes an enforcement tool when a broken proof fails a commit hook or a CI step instead of merely being recorded.

`provreq gate` re-verifies every requirement the committed baseline recorded as passing and exits non-zero if any no longer does. The baseline is the `verdicts.yml` committed at a chosen ref — `HEAD` by default, or a base branch in CI — read directly from git so a working copy a bad local run may already have overwritten cannot mask a regression; when no committed baseline can be read, the gate falls back to the recorded verdicts on disk and says so, enforcing against the last recorded state rather than refusing to run. Only a requirement that was passing can regress: one that was never proven has no proof to lose, and the gate guards proven ground rather than demanding that everything become proven.

A requirement regresses when its fresh outcome falls from holds — to unknown, to refuted, or to a state that can no longer produce a verdict at all, such as a formalization that is no longer admitted or no longer gates. That outcome regression always fails the gate. A trust-tier downgrade that still holds — a weaker basis (proven over model-checked over not-falsified), or a mechanical result replaced by an asserted human-tagged one — is a configurable additional failure, off by default, because a still-passing requirement has not fallen even though the ground under it has weakened.

The re-verify is the cache-served one (REQ091): a requirement whose world is unchanged is answered from its stored verdict rather than a fresh engine run, so the gate is fast enough to sit in a commit hook, which is the whole reason the incremental cache had to come first. The gate reports and blocks; it never auto-fixes or auto-drafts a regression — restoring the proof is a human decision.
